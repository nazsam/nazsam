Compliance & Governance

What is the difference between a standard, a framework, and a regulation?

Answer

Standard = specific requirements (ISO 27001 controls). Framework = structured guidance (NIST CSF). Regulation = legally enforceable (GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS).

What does "compliance as code" mean?

Answer

Automating control checks via policy engines and CI — continuous evidence instead of annual manual audits alone. See compliance-as-code.

Map NIST CSF functions in one sentence each.

Answer

Govern — risk strategy. Identify — assets and risks. Protect — safeguards. Detect — anomalies. Respond — incidents. Recover — resilience.

What is GDPR's data minimization principle?

Answer

Collect and retain only personal data adequate, relevant, and limited to the stated purpose — reduces breach impact and compliance scope.

What is a SOC 2 Type II report?

Answer

Independent auditor attestation that security controls operated effectively over a period (typically 6–12 months) — common vendor due diligence artifact.

How does ISO 27001 differ from NIST CSF?

Answer

ISO 27001 is certifiable ISMS standard with Annex A controls. NIST CSF is voluntary, flexible framework — often used together (CSF for structure, ISO for certification path).

What is continuous control monitoring?

Answer

Automated, near-real-time verification that controls remain effective — e.g., MFA enforced, encryption on, public buckets blocked — vs point-in-time assessments.

What is a risk register?

Answer

Living inventory of risks: likelihood, impact, owner, treatment (accept/mitigate/transfer/avoid), and status — foundation of governance programs.

What is third-party risk management (TPRM)?

Answer

Assessing vendors who access data/systems — questionnaires, SOC reports, penetration tests, contractual SLAs, and ongoing monitoring for supply-chain exposure.

Why map controls across frameworks?

Answer

Avoid duplicate work — one detective control (e.g., MFA) may satisfy ISO A.9, NIST PR.AC, CIS IG1, and PCI 8.3 simultaneously.
